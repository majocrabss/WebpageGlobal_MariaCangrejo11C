Do Educational Interventions Significantly Improve Student Scores? A Hypothesis Testing Approach in the Colombian Educational Context

Introduction

Educational interventions aimed at improving student performance are a central concern in Colombian educational policy. According to the Fundación Empresarios por la Educación (2024), persistent score gaps between student groups in the Saber 11 national exam reflect structural differences in access to quality education and the effectiveness of pedagogical strategies. At the international level, the OECD (2023) documents that targeted educational interventions can significantly reduce achievement gaps when properly designed and implemented. This report applies hypothesis testing to evaluate whether a treatment intervention produced a statistically significant improvement in student scores compared to a control group, using data from the three professor-provided CSV datasets.

Understanding whether observed score differences reflect real effects or sampling variability is essential for evidence-based educational decision-making (Suárez & García, 2024). All statistical calculations were performed using the interactive hypothesis testing tool developed in C8 and C9 of this project, available at: https://majocrabss.github.io/webpageGlobal_MariaCangrejo/

Research Question

Is the mean score of students in the Treatment group significantly higher than the mean score of students in the Control group, at a significance level of α = 0.05?

Hypotheses, Test Direction, and Significance Level

H₀: μ_Treatment = μ_Control    (no significant difference in means)
H₁: μ_Treatment > μ_Control    (Treatment group scores are higher)
Test direction: Right-tailed
Significance level: α = 0.05    Critical value: z* = 1.645

CSV Data Upload — How It Works

To use the CSV mode (C9 tab of the deployed webpage), the user clicks the upload zone or drags and drops a CSV file into it. The tool automatically reads the column headers and populates dropdown menus for the group column, value column (for mean tests) or success column (for proportion tests), benchmark group, and test group. The user selects the test direction and significance level, then clicks "Run Hypothesis Test." The tool computes summary statistics for each group, calculates the z-statistic, p-value, and critical value, displays the result on an interactive normal curve, and shows the statistical decision and a contextual conclusion.

Results

The following results were obtained using Dataset 1 (dataset_1.csv) in the C9 CSV mode, with the Control group as benchmark and the Treatment group as the test group, right-tailed, α = 0.05:

StatisticValue
Benchmark mean (Control, μ₀)70.50
Sample mean (Treatment, x̄)79.92
Standard deviation — Control (σ)2.75
Sample size — Treatment (n)12
Test statistic (z)11.88
P-value< 0.0001
Critical value (z*)1.645
Significance level (α)0.05
DecisionReject H₀

Since the p-value (< 0.0001) is substantially less than α = 0.05, and the test statistic z = 11.88 far exceeds the critical value z* = 1.645, we reject the null hypothesis.

Discussion

The statistical evidence strongly supports the alternative hypothesis: the Treatment group scored significantly higher than the Control group. This result is consistent with international research on the effectiveness of structured educational interventions. The OECD (2023) reports that well-designed pedagogical interventions produce measurable and statistically significant gains in student achievement, particularly when they address specific learning gaps through targeted support.

In the Colombian context, the Fundación Empresarios por la Educación (2024) documents that structured academic support programs have shown positive effects on Saber 11 results in participating institutions. At the research level, Suárez and García (2024) identified instructional quality and targeted pedagogical support as among the strongest predictors of mathematics performance in the Saber 11, reinforcing the practical importance of the kind of intervention evaluated in this report.

Statistical Errors in This Context

Type I Error (α = 0.05) Concluding the intervention improved scores when it actually did not. This could lead to scaling an ineffective program at significant cost to the educational system and to students.
Type II Error (β) Failing to detect a real improvement produced by the intervention. This would cause a genuinely effective program to be discontinued, denying students its measurable benefits.

Conclusion

At a significance level of α = 0.05, the hypothesis test provides very strong statistical evidence that the Treatment group scored significantly higher than the Control group (z = 11.88, p < 0.0001). The null hypothesis is rejected. The conclusion matches the statistical decision: the educational intervention had a real and substantial effect on student performance. These findings are consistent with international evidence on effective pedagogical strategies (OECD, 2023) and with Colombian research on the predictors of Saber 11 performance (Fundación Empresarios por la Educación, 2024; Suárez & García, 2024).
